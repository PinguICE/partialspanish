Resumen para el examen

Carbohydrates

Main function: provide energy.

Simple: monosaccharides (glucose, fructose, galactose, ribose) and disaccharides (lactose, maltose, saccharose).

Complex: oligosaccharides and polysaccharides (starch, glycogen, cellulose, chitin).

Proteins

Abundant organic molecules. Each protein has a specific structure and job.

  • Digestive enzymes: amylase, lipase, pepsin, trypsin.
  • Transport: hemoglobin, albumin.
  • Structural: actin, tubulin, keratin.
  • Hormones: insulin, thyroxine.
  • Defense: immunoglobulins.
  • Contractile: actin, myosin.
  • Storage: legume proteins, egg white.

Lipids

Hydrophobic molecules; do not mix with water.

Functions: long-term energy storage, insulation, waterproofing, membranes, temperature control, food texture and flavor, and hormone production.

Types: fats and oils, waxes, phospholipids, acylglycerides, steroids and carotenoids.

Vitamins

Fat-soluble: A, D, E, K. Dissolve in fat and can be stored.

Water-soluble: B-complex and C. Dissolve in water and are not stored in large amounts.

Examples: A—vision; C—collagen and iron absorption; D—calcium absorption; K—blood clotting.

Minerals

  • Iron (Fe): hemoglobin carries oxygen.
  • Calcium (Ca): bones, teeth, muscle contraction.
  • Potassium (K): fluid balance, nerve signals, muscles.
  • Iodine (I): thyroid hormones.
  • Zinc (Zn): immunity, growth, wound healing.
  • Magnesium (Mg): muscles, nerves, enzyme activity.

Important exam connections

  • Vitamin C helps the body absorb iron.
  • Vitamin D helps the body absorb calcium.
  • Iron is part of hemoglobin, which carries oxygen.
  • Calcium, potassium and magnesium support muscle function.
  • Iodine is needed to make thyroid hormones.
